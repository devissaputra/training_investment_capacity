# Research Design

## Decision problem

Training capacity is often discussed as if participation and investment were interchangeable.

They are not.

An organization or sector may train a large share of its workforce while allocating relatively little real expenditure per employee or trainee.

This study separates those dimensions using a repeated official UK employer series.

## Portfolio framing

Learning & Development Research / Workforce Development / Training Investment.

## Source

UK Employer Skills Survey 2024 — Investment in Training.

Official Explore Education Statistics dataset:

- 7,848 rows;
- 2011–2024 coverage;
- national and regional levels;
- sector and site-size filters;
- source-provided 2024-price expenditure fields.

## Design

Repeated cross-sectional secondary analysis of published aggregate official statistics.

The study is not longitudinal at the employer level.

Different survey samples contribute to different waves.

## Comparable UK waves

2011, 2013, 2015, 2017, 2022, and 2024.

2019 is omitted from the UK trend because Scotland did not participate.

## Evidence layers

The package separates four evidence layers:

1. real spend per employee;
2. real spend per trainee and total expenditure;
3. training reach;
4. sector distribution.

## Survey-production context

The 2024 Investment in Training follow-up retained 5,935 complete sites from 6,210 achieved interviews.

Published expenditure estimates also incorporate survey weighting and modelling of missing expenditure components.

The repository consumes the official aggregate results and does not reproduce the microdata production process.

## Construct model

**Investment intensity**

Observed through real expenditure per employee and per trainee.

**Reach**

Observed approximately through the repository-derived trainees/employees ratio and interpreted alongside official ESS participation statistics.

**Training capacity**

Used only as an L&D resource-deployment framing that combines reach and investment intensity.

It is not treated as a directly measured latent construct.

## Sector comparison

All 13 published UK sectors are included.

Sector differences are interpreted descriptively because workforce mix, firm size, regulatory obligations, occupations, and delivery models can differ structurally across sectors.

## Validity boundary

The design does not estimate:

- training effectiveness;
- transfer;
- skill gain;
- causal ROI;
- employee-level treatment exposure;
- organizational learning capability.
